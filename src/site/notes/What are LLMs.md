## What is an LLM (Large Language Model)?

A **Large Language Model (LLM)** is an advanced AI system trained on vast amounts of written text to process, understand, and generate human language.

LLMs process human language by:

- **Massive Datasets:** Training on trillions of words from books, articles, code, and websites.
    
- **Context Recognition:** Mapping out how concepts, words, and grammar relate to one another.
    
- **Statistical Generation:** Calculating the most mathematically probable words to send back as a response.

Large Language Models (LLMs) are advanced artificial intelligence systems trained on vast amounts of text data to comprehend, generate, and translate human language.

**Popular Examples of LLMs**

- **GPT Series (OpenAI):** Powers advanced chat interfaces, automated code completion, and complex reasoning applications.
    
- **Gemini (Google):** Multimodal models designed to natively process text, code, images, audio, and video simultaneously.
    
- **Claude (Anthropic):** Recognized for precise long-form text analysis, safe coding assistance, and nuanced contextual awareness.
    
- **Llama (Meta):** Open-weights models that allow developers to download, modify, and run the AI locally on their own infrastructure.
    

**Examples of What LLMs Can Do**

- **Text & Code Generation:** Writing software in Python or JavaScript, drafting professional emails, or composing creative narratives.
    
- **Document Summarization:** Distilling lengthy research papers, financial reports, or meeting transcripts into concise bullet points.
    
- **Language Translation:** Converting sentences and entire documents across dozens of global languages with high contextual accuracy.
    
- **Conversational Problem Solving:** Acting as a virtual tutor to break down complex math equations, debug programming errors, or brainstorm project ideas.
  
  
  ## What is a Vector?

At its simplest, a **vector** is a list of numbers that represents a point or a direction in a multi-dimensional space.

- **In Math & Physics:** A vector represents both magnitude (length) and direction (e.g., traveling 50 mph North).
    
- **In Machine Learning & AI:** Vectors are used to represent complex data—like words, images, or audio—as numerical coordinates so computers can process them.
