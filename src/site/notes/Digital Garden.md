A **digital garden** is an online, interactive space where an individual cultivates ideas, notes, and knowledge over time. It is a hybrid between a personal blog, a private notebook, and a wiki.

Unlike traditional blogs—which publish finished articles in chronological order—a digital garden prioritizes **topography over timelines** and **process over polish**. Notes are interlinked based on how concepts relate to one another, and they are continuously updated as the author learns more.

## Blog vs. Digital Garden

|**Feature**|**Traditional Blog**|**Digital Garden**|
|---|---|---|
|**Organization**|Chronological (newest posts at the top).|Topographical (networked by topic and interlinks).|
|**Maturity**|Articles are "finished" once published.|Notes are living documents (rough ideas evolve into evergreen content).|
|**Tone**|Formal, polished, performance-oriented.|Imperfect, exploratory, "learning in public".|
|**Mindset**|Broadcast (sending an essay to readers).|Cultivation (tending to personal notes that others can explore).|

## Core Principles: How a Digital Garden Works

Digital gardens follow several key design principles and mechanics:

### 1. Plant Ideas early ("Seedlings" to "Evergreens")

Notes aren't saved until they are perfect essays. Instead, authors publish raw thoughts and tag them with their stage of growth (often called **epistemic status**):

- 🌱 **Seedling:** A rough, unrefined note or half-formed thought.
    
- 🌿 **Budding:** A note with structure, research, and some connections, but still developing.
    
- 🌳 **Evergreen:** A well-developed, heavily interconnected concept that serves as a core piece of knowledge.
    

### 2. Dense Internal & Bi-directional Links

Rather than forcing content into rigid folders, notes are connected to related concepts using hyperlinks (often **bi-directional links**). If Note A links to Note B, Note B automatically shows a reference back to Note A. This allows readers to wander non-linearly through topics based on curiosity.

### 3. Continuous Tending & Revision

Like a real garden, digital gardens require maintenance. When the author reads a new book, learns a new skill, or changes their mind, they go back to old notes to refine definitions, prune outdated ideas, or link them to new concepts.

### 4. Learning in Public

Digital gardens remove the friction and anxiety of "having to be right" before sharing work. By publishing thought processes openly, gardeners encourage feedback, collaboration, and collective learning.

## Common Tools Used to Build Digital Gardens

Digital gardens are typically constructed using Markdown-based personal knowledge management (PKM) software combined with web publishing frameworks:

- **Note-Taking / Storage:** [Obsidian](https://obsidian.md/), Logseq, Roam Research, or Notion.
    
- **Publishing Frameworks:** Obsidian Publish, Quartz, Astro, Next.js, Hugo, or Gatsby.
    
- **Visual Graph Views:** Interactive 2D visual node maps that let readers see how notes link to each other visually.
